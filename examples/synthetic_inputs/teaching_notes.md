# Teaching Notes — Introductory Physics (synthetic)

Original notes written for the ExamDNA demonstration.

## Uniformly accelerated motion (K1, K2)
- Use v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as. Choose the equation that does not contain the quantity you neither know nor need.
- "Comes to rest" means v = 0 — a boundary condition students often miss.
- On a velocity–time graph the gradient is acceleration and the area under the line is displacement.
- Common error: using the final speed as the average speed.

## Projectiles (K3)
- Horizontal and vertical motions are independent. An object leaving a table horizontally starts with zero vertical velocity.
- Time of fall depends only on height: t = √(2h/g).

## Forces (F1–F3)
- Newton's first law: no resultant force ⇒ constant velocity (which includes being at rest).
- Lift problems: T − mg = ma when accelerating upward. At constant speed T = mg.
- Hooke's law holds only up to the limit of proportionality.

## Energy and power (E1, E2)
- Energy lost from the gravitational store = energy gained in the kinetic store when air resistance is negligible: v = √(2gh), independent of mass.
- Efficiency is a ratio — no unit — and cannot exceed 1.
- Minimum power estimates ignore other energy transfers; say so in answers.

## Momentum (M1)
- Momentum is a vector: choose a positive direction before adding momenta.
- In a collision where objects stick together, momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not.

## Waves (W1, W2)
- v = fλ. Frequency is set by the source and does not change on refraction; speed and wavelength do.
- v = c/n for light in a medium of refractive index n.

## Electricity (C1, C2)
- Series circuits: same current everywhere; resistances add; potential differences share in proportion to resistance.
- I = Q/t, P = VI, E = Pt. Thicker cables have lower resistance and heat less for a given current.
